What 20 passes bought

Every one of the 1,000 individual LLM passes behind run 498b3a7d was recovered from the MLflow traces and scored on its own. This is what the TP/FP/TN/FN look like pass by pass — and what the 20-way vote did and didn't buy.

How these numbers exist at all

reconstruction verified

The run report only stores the aggregated vote (vote_counts per pair), so per-pass confusion matrices were not recoverable from it. They were rebuilt from mlflow.db: exactly 1,000 traces carry a doc_id tag — 20 per document for all 50 documents — and each root span holds the full model output. Every output was re-parsed with main.py's own parse_llm_json, then re-aggregated through utils.resample.aggregate_run_triples and utils.reporting.

The rebuild reproduces the logged artifact exactly: 4,067 pair rows, zero vote_counts mismatches, zero prediction mismatches, and binary/binary_intra/binary_inter identical to 16 digits. Nothing below is an estimate of the run — it is the run, disaggregated.

One caveat on pass identity

constrain_to_pair_list is false for ESL, so no pair list is enumerated in the prompt and reshuffle_per_resample has nothing to reshuffle — all 20 prompts for a document are byte-identical. Pass index is therefore arbitrary (ordered here by trace start time) and carries no shared factor across documents. That does not affect any distribution below; it only means "pass 7" is a label, not an experimental condition. It also means every difference you see between passes at temperature 0 is pure backend nondeterminism.

The confusion matrices, pass by pass

intra

Counts are over the real candidate space — every ordered mention pair (a,b), a≠b in the 50 documents, which is how pair_list_ids is built. That is 25,742 directed pairs, 4,038 of them intra-sentence, with 2,020 gold positives (598 intra). TP, FP and FN are identical to what the run report would give; only TN differs, for reasons in the last section.

The spread is not subtle. FP swings by 224 between the leanest and the most talkative pass — a third of its own mean — while TP swings by 70. Precision and recall move in opposite directions across passes and very nearly cancel: the correlation between a pass's output volume and its recall is +0.74, with precision at −0.50, leaving F1 essentially uncorrelated with volume (+0.03). Each pass is a different point on the same trade-off curve, not a better or worse classifier drawn from one distribution.

The binary view adds the 21,704 inter-sentence pairs, of which 1,422 are gold positive. Since the prompt forbids answering them, they are almost all FN by construction — FN barely moves (cv 1.1%) because it is dominated by a fixed block of ~1,400 unanswerable pairs.

Majority vote is the wrong threshold

A quieter bug in the aggregation

aggregate_run_triples takes a 3-way argmax over {causes, causedby, norel}, with norel votes implied by absence. A pair split 6 causes / 5 causedby / 9 norel therefore resolves to norel even though 11 of 20 passes called it causal. That happened to 8 pairs here. All 8 were gold-negative, so this run got lucky — direction splitting bought +0.0023 F1 instead of costing recall. It is still an unintended coupling between the directional decision and the binary one; data.vote_to_binary exists to remove it and is currently off.

How many passes were actually needed

5

At $0.052 per pass, the run spent $0.78 on passes 6 through 20 to move intra F1 by −0.001. The one thing the extra passes do buy is a finer-grained vote count, which is what makes the k≥6 threshold and the ranking below possible — but 8–10 passes would give nearly the same resolution.

Are the passes really different, or just noisy?

systematically different

If each pair fired independently in each pass with its own rate pi = ki/20, the variance of a pass's positive count would be Σ pi(1−pi). It isn't close.

  • 13.2×Overdispersion in output volume. Observed variance of intra predictions per pass is 4,190 against 318 expected under independent per-pair noise (χ² = 250.5 on 19 df, p ≈ 2×10⁻⁴²). One pass emits 848 positives, another 1,118. The mechanism is document-level clustering, not a persistent per-pass temperament — see the dependency section below.
  • 11.9×…concentrated in false positives. FP variance is 2,889 vs 242 expected. TP is much better behaved at 3.9× — the extra talk lands mostly on negatives.
  • 123.5Cochran's Q over the 20 passes (19 df, p ≈ 2×10⁻¹⁷): the passes are not equally accurate. 88 of the 190 pass pairs differ at p<0.05 by McNemar; worst vs. best pass gives 358/261 discordant pairs, p = 0.0001.
  • 0.55Fleiss κ across the 20 passes on intra pairs (0.64 on binary). Mean pairwise Cohen κ is 0.550 ± 0.032. Two passes share only 49% of their positive sets by Jaccard.
What this means for the resampling design

These are not 20 draws from one classifier with per-pair jitter. Majority voting over correlated voters is exactly the regime where voting underdelivers — which is what the +0.017 measures. What the correlation is between turns out to be the important question, and it is answered next: not between passes, but between the pairs inside a document.

Pairs are not decided independently

binary_intra

Everything in this section is intra-sentence only, and collapsed to undirected decisions for the reason immediately below. The null throughout is a permutation that keeps each decision's vote count and destroys coupling — shuffle the pass axis independently per pair.

The mirror is one decision wearing two rows

Of 2,019 undirected intra pairs × 20 passes = 40,380 direction-decisions, exactly 5 were asymmetric. φ between mirrored directions is 0.9991 (median 1.000), agreement 0.9999. The prompt's symmetry rule is obeyed essentially deterministically, so the 4,038 intra rows are 2,019 decisions and support_pos = 598 is 299 links. F1 is unaffected — precision and recall both scale — but every count, every χ², every confidence interval is on half the independent units it looks like.

Inside a document, decisions travel together

One document, every decision, all 20 passes

1_15ecbplus.xml has 20 candidate intra-sentence relations spread over four sentences — small enough to print whole. Twelve of the twenty vary across passes. Read the grid first: each row is a pass, each column a relation, a filled cell means that pass called it causal.

The sentence is the unit, but the event is not

  • 10.3×Per-document counts are over-dispersed. Summed over documents, the observed variance of positives per pass is 1,642 against 159 under independence. Median per-document ratio 3.7; the worst is 1_13ecbplus.xml, which emits between 3 and 70 of its 73 candidate decisions depending on the pass (32×). 1_12ecbplus runs 3→46, 3_4ecbplus 8→49.
  • 16.2%Sentences behave like switches. In sentences with ≥3 candidates, every decision is off in 16.2% of (sentence, pass) cells against 13.4% under independence, and every decision is on in 3.85% against 2.14%. Both tails are inflated.
  • 1.04×But there is no hub-event effect. The variance-to-mean ratio of mention degree in a pass's predicted graph is 0.857, against 0.825 for a random selection of the same size from the same document. The model is not deciding "this event is causal" — the clustering is regional, not per-mention.

Errors: same document yes, same pass no

Two different questions hide behind "are the errors always in the same runs".

  • p=0.20There is no bad pass. Kendall's W over the 50 documents, against a null that re-aligns pass labels at random: error count W = 0.087 vs null 0.075 (z = +0.8, p = 0.196); F1 W = 0.085 vs 0.075 (p = 0.26). Only output volume clears the null (W = 0.109 vs 0.068, p = 0.006), and that traces to a faint start-time effect (r = +0.081 between a pass's start order within its document and its volume, p = 0.022) — scheduling, worth 0.6% of variance. Pass identity does not survive from one document to the next; cross-document covariance is in fact negative (−600 against +1,642 within).
  • −1/19No error personality either. The 20×20 pass correlation matrix over per-decision errors averages −0.0526, which is exactly the −1/(k−1) centering artifact — no structure. Its leading eigenvalue is 1.81 against a ~1.29 noise bound: one weak factor worth 9.1% of pass-space variance (noise alone gives ~5%), loading 0.55 on output volume.
  • +0.37Within a document, errors absolutely co-occur. FP-with-FP correlation is +0.261 in the same document and +0.368 in the same sentence (TP-with-TP: +0.182 / +0.303). 87.8% of false positives in multi-candidate sentences arrive with company — 5,748 against 795 arriving alone.
  • 0.58Misses are stabler than false alarms. FN-set Jaccard between passes is 0.576 against 0.455 for FP sets. The model reliably fails to see the same links; what fluctuates is how much extra it invents. This is why voting cannot clean up — the co-firing errors co-fire in most passes, which is the 46% of FP mass sitting at k ≥ 16.
What this costs the headline interval

Bootstrapping the 20-vote F1 of 0.399 by different units: directed rows as logged (4,038) sd 0.0168, CI [0.367, 0.432] · undirected decisions (2,019) sd 0.0240, CI [0.350, 0.447] · sentences (221) sd 0.0268 · documents (50) sd 0.0281, CI [0.344, 0.454]. Design effect 2.78×; effective n ≈ 1,450, not 4,038. Two configurations differing by less than about 0.06 F1 are not separable on 50 documents, and any pair-level significance test between runs will manufacture differences that are not there. Cluster by document.

What the vote count is worth as a score

AUC 0.769
  • 77%of gold intra positives live in the contested zone (1–19 votes). Only 14% are found unanimously, and 9% (54 pairs) are never proposed by any pass — that is the hard recall ceiling of 0.910 for anything built on these votes.
  • 55%of gold-negative intra pairs get at least one causal vote (1,880 of 3,440). A single pass's ~679 false positives are drawn from that pool, but not uniformly: 46% of all FP mass comes from pairs that 16+ passes agree on. Voting cannot remove those.

Three things that distort the headline numbers

read before quoting

1 · binary is mostly measuring pairs the prompt forbids answering

70.4% of gold positives (1,422 of 2,020) are inter-sentence, and the system prompt says "do NOT output a relation for that pair under any circumstances." A perfect intra-only system would score binary R = 0.296, P = 1.0, F1 = 0.457. The logged 0.2016 is 44% of that ceiling, not 20% of a reachable 1.0. binary_intra is the only headline here that means what it looks like.

Note also that the model violates the scope rule in every pass: 27.5 inter-sentence predictions per pass on average (range 10–50). Those violations are 50.5% precise — considerably better than its 30.9% precision on the intra pairs it was asked about. When it volunteers an out-of-scope answer, it is usually onto something.

2 · The TN in the report is not a corpus TN

reconstruct_pairwise_predictions builds its universe as gold ∪ predicted ∪ voted, so the only negatives in the 4,067 logged rows are ones some pass voted for. Real candidate space is 25,742 pairs (4,038 intra). For the intra ensemble that is TN = 1,431 in the report universe vs 2,930 in reality — accuracy would read 0.673 instead of 0.794. Precision, recall and F1 are unaffected; accuracy, specificity, MCC and anything else touching TN are not comparable to published numbers computed over a fixed candidate set.

3 · Direction is close to a coin flip

Across all 20 passes, of the 6,069 intra pairs correctly identified as causal, only 59.6% carry the correct arrow. That single factor is most of the gap between binary_intra F1 0.401 and the 3-class micro_f1 of 0.118. Any effort spent on the causal/norel decision is worth roughly half as much as it looks until direction improves.

Pass noise is not your biggest uncertainty

1.9×

Bootstrapping the 50 documents (2,000 resamples) puts the ensemble intra F1 at 0.399 with a 95% CI of [0.343, 0.455], sd 0.029. Pass-to-pass sd is 0.015. Document sampling contributes about twice the uncertainty that resampling does, and the per-document instability is severe in its own right: mean within-document F1 sd across passes is 0.099, and on the smallest documents a single pass swings between 0.0 and 1.0.

Put bluntly: the 20× compute went into shrinking the smaller of the two error bars. The same budget spent on 200 documents with 3 passes each would have produced a materially tighter headline.

What I would change

actionable
  • 1Drop to 5–8 passes and spend the saved calls on documents. Nothing in the F1 curve justifies 20; the vote resolution argument tops out around 8–10.
  • 2Log per-pass predictions. aggregate_run_triples already builds a votes list in run order and main.py keeps pair_stats — but only vote_counts survives into the report. Persisting the ordered votes would make this whole analysis a one-liner instead of a trace-mining exercise.
  • 3Make the vote threshold a tuned parameter, selected per fold on the training half (the 5-fold split already exists in the config). Worth +0.028 F1 honestly measured, versus 0 for hard-coded majority.
  • 4Turn on vote_to_binary — or at least measure with it — so the directional split cannot silently veto a binary majority.
  • 5Quote binary_intra, and only binary_intra. The framework stays as it is — inter-sentence pairs remain in the pipeline for their own reasons — but binary, micro_f1 and macro_f1 from an intra-only prompt are ceiling-limited artefacts (0.457 max on binary) and should not be compared against anything.
  • 6Cluster by document in every comparison. Design effect 2.78× on the F1 interval; effective n ≈ 1,450 against 4,038 logged rows. A pair-level test between two configurations will report differences that a document-level bootstrap does not support. The practical threshold on 50 documents is about 0.06 F1.
  • 7Count undirected decisions when reporting support. The mirrored rows are one decision (φ = 0.999), so 598 gold intra positives are 299 links. This changes no metric value and every degrees-of-freedom.
  • 8Treat TN-based numbers as offline recomputations. Accuracy, specificity and MCC over the report's gold ∪ pred ∪ voted universe are not comparable across runs; recompute them over the fixed candidate space when they are needed, rather than reading them off the artifact.
